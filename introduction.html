Who is JhoneVic?

JhoneVic M. Casaña is a diarist, observer, and chronicler of his own life—a quiet witness to the ordinary and extraordinary moments that shape a person from adolescence into adulthood.

Born and raised in the Philippines, he began writing in 2013 at the age of 14, never imagining that his personal notebook entries would one day become a documented record of nearly a decade and a half of growth, heartbreak, resilience, and self-discovery.

The Diaries

JhoneVic's diaries are not polished memoirs or literary exercises. They are raw, unedited, and deeply honest accounts of daily life—written in a natural mix of Filipino and English, often switching between languages mid-sentence, filled with slang, self-deprecating humor, and the unfiltered voice of a young person navigating the chaos of growing up.

The collection spans three distinct periods:

2013–2014 (Ages 14–16): The early years. These diaries capture the intense, world-ending emotions of first love, first heartbreak, and the turbulence of teenage life. At the center is his complicated feelings for a classmate—a relationship that would define much of his emotional landscape during these years.

He writes about school, classmates, family, and a local computer shop where he would often go to catch glimpses of her. The tone is dramatic, playful, and deeply earnest— a young person learning what it means to love, lose, and hope.

2026 (Age 27–28): The adult years. The diary returns after a long silence, and the voice has matured. JhoneVic is now navigating the complexities of adulthood, rebuilding relationships, managing the challenges of daily life, and discovering new perspectives on love, family, anxiety, social awkwardness, and loneliness.

Key Themes

First Love and Heartbreak: JhoneVic's early diaries capture the intensity of first love— the longing, jealousy, hope, rejection, and emotional whiplash of being a teenager in love for the first time.

Family and Belonging: Throughout the diaries, family is both a source of comfort and frustration. Relationships evolve, circumstances change, and different periods of living with relatives become formative experiences.

Mental Health and Self-Awareness: The 2026 diary marks a significant shift toward greater self-reflection. The diary becomes a place to examine emotions, uncertainty, relationships, and the challenges of adulthood day by day.

The Act of Writing Itself: Perhaps the most profound theme is JhoneVic's relationship with his own diary. At 14, it was an emotional outlet. By adulthood, it had become a tool for self-analysis—a mirror reflecting how much a person can change while still remaining recognizably themselves.

Final Words

JhoneVic is not a famous author, a public figure, or a polished storyteller. He is simply someone who chose to write down his life—the good days and the bad, the moments of joy and the nights of loneliness, the crushes that faded and the connections that endured.

His diaries are a testament to the power of writing: not as a performance, but as a way to understand oneself. They are proof that the stories of ordinary people, told with honesty and vulnerability, can be just as compelling as any epic.

He is a keeper of fragments—a collector of moments—and through his words, we are invited to witness the quiet, messy, and deeply human process of becoming who we are.

JhoneVic continues to write. His diaries are preserved and transcribed as a personal archive—a legacy of honesty, growth, and the enduring belief that our stories, no matter how small, deserve to be told.
